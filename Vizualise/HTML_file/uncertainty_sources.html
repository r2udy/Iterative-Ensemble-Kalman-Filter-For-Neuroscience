Two sources of uncertainty in
static data assimilation

In a static problem, the "model uncertainty" is a prior covariance matrix B — it encodes how spread out our initial knowledge is. The observation uncertainty is R. The analysis is a weighted average of the two.

The two distributions meet core concept

Prior (B) and observation (R) are two independent sources of knowledge. Their combination — the posterior — is always narrower than either alone.

The two extreme cases limiting behaviour

The relative size of B and R determines where the analysis lands. The Kalman gain K ∈ [0, 1] is the fraction of the gap that is closed toward the observation.

B ≫ R  ·  uncertain prior → trust the obs
R ≫ B  ·  noisy obs → trust the prior
In 2D — the covariance matrix as an ellipse joint uncertainty

In higher dimensions, B and R are matrices. Their shapes (ellipses) encode not just spread but also correlations between parameters. The posterior ellipse is always strictly inside both.

Obs. error covariance  R

Ensemble reading — members carry B, observation carries R ensemble view

Each ensemble member is one draw from the prior (B). The observation arrives with its own uncertainty (R). The update moves members toward the observation — more so when B is large relative to R.
